## Decisions and controls needed

1. **Define the operator and roles per flow.** Distinguish a pilot using a device-local tool for private purposes, MoFlight operating accounts/AI/support, and a club using it for member administration. Software authorship alone does not settle controllership; local storage alone does not settle it either. Document who determines purposes and means and when MoFlight acts for a club. Controller/processor relationships require the appropriate allocation and contracts. The personal/household exemption is narrow and is not a blanket exemption for a publicly offered service. [EDPB roles](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/learn-the-basics/data-controller-or-data-processor_en), [GDPR Art. 2 and Recital 18](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng).

2. **Create one processing inventory.** For each field/flow record purpose, affected people, source, legal basis, recipients, storage, retention and safeguards. Cover own flights, instructors/passengers, duty partners, Medical, photos/free text, device/session identifiers and service logs. Necessary service processing may fit a contract; legitimate interests need a documented balancing assessment; consent must be specific and withdrawable. A pilot's click cannot supply another person's consent. A pilot's legal logbook duty does not automatically become MoFlight's legal obligation. Assess provider/club copying and reuse conditions separately from GDPR. [EDPB lawful processing](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/process-personal-data-lawfully_en).

3. **Minimize capture and disclosure.** Specify needed columns before broad history/club retrieval; preserve only justified raw evidence. Retain the accepted evidence-first architecture, while defining lifecycle boundaries: provenance preservation does not require indefinite retention. Necessary instructor names can differ from unnecessary club-wide history. Initials and hashed IDs may remain identifiable. Specify audience and export rules under #15. For photos, propose user cropping, metadata removal and a preview of the material leaving the device.

4. **Separate disconnect from erasure.** Offer clear meanings for provider sign-out, forgetting credentials, deleting a book, deleting all local data and later deleting a cloud account. Erasure design must account for captures, revisions, recovery journals/safety copies, old restored databases, SQLite side files, photos, import caches, generated JSON/print files and queues. Define backup expiry and prevent restored/cloud copies from silently recreating erased records. Explain that user-exported copies and the source club's records are outside a local-delete button's control. Establish any actual statutory retention exceptions rather than inventing a universal flight-record period. Provide access/correction/export/objection/restriction/erasure handling as applicable; requests normally require a response within one month. [EDPB rights](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/respect-individuals-rights_en).

5. **Verify security on both platforms.** Assess device loss, shared devices, account switching, exported files, WebView bridges/cookies and diagnostic logs. Keep secrets out of backups; verify local clearing and credential lifecycle, including reinstall. Choose proportionate encryption/key handling and export protection. Test two accounts, foreign IDs, expired sessions and offline restart using synthetic data. A SecureStore password or Android pass does not prove database, WebView, backup or iOS protection. GDPR requires risk-appropriate measures; a particular database-encryption product is not universally mandated. [GDPR Arts. 25 and 32](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng).

6. **Give Medical its own gate.** Treat validity/expiry information as potentially revealing health status, even without a diagnosis. Determine whether the actual fields are health data; if so, establish an Article 6 basis and an Article 9 exception, such as valid explicit consent where appropriate. Propose storing only the user's necessary reminder information, with no certificate image or general AI access by default. [EDPB sensitive-data conditions](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/process-personal-data-lawfully_en).

7. **Gate cloud, external AI and SDKs before activation.** Map every recipient, including hosting, email, crash reporting and support. Where processors are involved, obtain AVVs/DPAs, review subprocessors, deletion/retention and AI training use. Verify actual region, support access and transfer safeguards; an EU database location alone does not settle international access. A user choosing AI is a product action, not a substitute for a lawful basis for every included person's data. Propose bounded excerpts and excluding credentials/Medical/unneeded names. [EDPB processors](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/learn-the-basics/data-controller-or-data-processor_en), [international transfers](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/international-data-transfers_en).

8. **Prepare notices and operations.** Provide clear German information before relevant processing, controller/contact and complaint details, purposes/bases, recipients/transfers, retention and rights. Assess Article 14 duties for people whose data comes from clubs/photos; do not assume the pilot alone is the data subject. Screen youth/student use: Article 8's parental-consent rule is conditional on consent-based information-society services offered directly to children, not a universal ban on under-16 users. Review TDDDG §25 for device storage/access, especially optional SDKs; strictly necessary requested-service storage has an exception, so no blanket consent banner is proposed. Document TOMs, processing records as applicable, DPIA/DSFA screening and DPO/DSB appointment assessment. High-risk processing needs a DPIA before starting. Prepare an incident procedure: notify the authority within 72 hours of awareness where required, and affected people for high-risk breaches. [GDPR Arts. 8, 13–14 and 30–37](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng), [TDDDG §25 official text](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.juris.de/ttdsg/__25.html), [EDPB DPIA guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/topics/accountability-and-compliance-tools/data-protection-impact-assessment_en), [EDPB breaches](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/assess-the-risks/data-breaches_en).

## Proposed acceptance sequence

Before distributing a real-data beta: settle audience/operator, inventory and lawful bases; verify permitted provider/member scope, retention/deletion, storage/export/session protection and notices/rights handling. These remain needed even for a small beta.

Before enabling Medical, external photo/AI or cloud flows: complete their additional legal/processor/transfer and native/isolation gates. A lower-exposure first release could remain local, with user-triggered retrieval and no external processing; that is a recommendation for Manuel to select.

Obtain a targeted German privacy-law review of the operator model, other members' data, Medical and youth scope before release. No compliance claim, retention period or legal basis is adopted by this review.
